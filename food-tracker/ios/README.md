# Food Tracker for iPhone

The food tracker as a real iPhone app. It shows the same web app (food.pivotaiglobal.com) and adds one
thing a website can't do: it reads Apple Health and sends your Watch's calories and steps on its own,
in the background. No Shortcut, no Sync button to remember.

## How it works

- **The app** is a full-screen view of the web app. You sign in once with the emailed code, like in Safari.
- **Turning on sync** (Goals > Apple Watch > Turn on automatic sync) makes a sync key, hands it to the app
  (stored in the iPhone's Keychain), and asks once for Health access.
- **Syncing.** The app sends the last 7 days to `POST /api/watch/sync` (`days` list) whenever Apple
  Health gets new samples (HealthKit background delivery, about hourly), on a background refresh, and
  every time the app opens. A day the phone missed catches up on the next sync.
- Health data is locked while the phone is locked, so syncs happen around unlocks. That's normal.
- Turning it on replaces the old Apple Shortcut: its key stops working, so delete the Shortcut and its
  automation.

Files: `FoodTracker/FoodTrackerApp.swift` (app, background refresh), `HealthSync.swift` (reading Health
and sending), `WebView.swift` (the web app, and the bridge it talks to: `web/src/native.ts`),
`Keychain.swift`, and `project.yml` (the Xcode project, generated by XcodeGen).

## One-time setup on your Mac

1. **Install Xcode** from the Mac App Store (free, large). Open it once and let it install its parts.
2. **Join the Apple Developer Program** ($99 a year) at developer.apple.com/programs with your Apple ID.
   It's needed for Health background sync and for family to install through TestFlight.
3. **Install XcodeGen** in Terminal: `brew install xcodegen` (Homebrew: brew.sh, if you don't have it).
4. **Get the code** in Terminal:
   ```
   git clone https://github.com/askokes/andrewkokes.git
   cd andrewkokes/food-tracker/ios
   xcodegen
   open FoodTracker.xcodeproj
   ```
5. **Sign it.** In Xcode, click **FoodTracker** at the top left, then the **FoodTracker** target, then
   **Signing & Capabilities**. Tick **Automatically manage signing** and pick your **Team**. Check that
   **HealthKit** shows with **Background Delivery** ticked.

## Put it on your iPhone

1. Plug the iPhone into the Mac (or same Wi-Fi after the first time). On the phone, allow
   **Developer Mode** if asked (Settings > Privacy & Security > Developer Mode, then restart).
2. In Xcode, pick your iPhone at the top, then press **▶ Run**.
3. On the phone: sign in, go to **Goals > Apple Watch**, tap **Turn on automatic sync**, and allow
   all three Health categories.
4. Delete the old Food Sync Shortcut and its automation in the Shortcuts app.

## Share it with family (TestFlight)

1. At appstoreconnect.apple.com: **Apps > + > New App**. Platform iOS, name **Food Tracker** (or any free
   name), bundle ID **com.pivotaiglobal.foodtracker**, SKU **foodtracker**.
2. In Xcode: pick **Any iOS Device** at the top, then **Product > Archive**. When it finishes, click
   **Distribute App > App Store Connect > Upload**.
3. In App Store Connect: **TestFlight**. Add family as **internal testers** (Users and Access first, with
   the Developer or Marketing role) for no review wait, or as **external testers** by email (Apple reviews
   the first build, usually a day).
4. They install **TestFlight** from the App Store and accept the invite.

For a new version: raise **CURRENT_PROJECT_VERSION** in `project.yml`, run `xcodegen`, archive and
upload again. Changes to the web app itself need no new build: the app shows the live site.

## If something's off

- **Numbers stop coming in:** iPhone Settings > Health > Data Access & Devices > Food Tracker, turn
  everything on. The Goals card shows the last sync and any error.
- **"Sign-in page" error on sync:** the sync path's Access bypass is missing; the deploy workflow's
  "Let the Apple Watch Shortcut through Access" step sets it.
- This code was written without a Mac to build it on. If Xcode shows an error, copy it to Andrew's
  Claude session.
